How bridge-day planning works

Bridge-day planning is the simplest way to turn a few vacation days into a much longer break: you place paid time off around public holidays and weekends so the non-working days in between connect into one longer stretch。

A concrete example: if a public holiday falls on a Thursday, taking the Friday off creates a four-day weekend using just one vacation day。 If that Thursday sits next to another holiday or a weekend, the same principle stacks further — three PTO days can become nine consecutive days off, as shown in our Netherlands 2027 guide。

The planner automates this search: it scans the full year, finds every workday "bridge" between clusters of non-working days, scores each by how many days off it produces per vacation day used, and applies the best options until your day budget runs out。

Frequently asked questions

Is Vacation Optimizer free?

Yes。 The planner, the country showcase, and all guides are free to use with no account required。 Your planner settings stay in your browser unless you share a link yourself。

What is a bridge day?

A bridge day is a vacation day placed between a public holiday (or weekend) and another day off, connecting them into one longer break。 For example, taking Friday off when Thursday is a public holiday turns a long weekend into a four-day break using a single PTO day。

How accurate is the holiday data?

Holiday data comes from public official sources per country and year, and the planner marks dates you can verify against your local calendar。 Holidays can change with short notice, so treat the planner as a planning aid and confirm final dates with your employer or local authority before booking。

Can I add my own office closures and ignore holidays that don't apply?

Yes。 The planner supports custom free days (company shutdowns, school schedules, or personal days) and lets you ignore specific public holidays that do not apply to your team or region, so the suggestions match your real calendar。

Does it work for part-time or shift workers?

The planner works with calendar days and assumes weekends are non-working days by default。 If your schedule differs, add your non-working dates as custom free days and ignore the default weekend handling where needed — and always confirm the plan against your employer's policy。

How do I get started?
