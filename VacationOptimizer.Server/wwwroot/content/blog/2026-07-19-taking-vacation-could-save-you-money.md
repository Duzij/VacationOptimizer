  We all know that taking a vacation is important for our well-being. Yet many of us postpone it or save our PTO "for later." But, as always, knowing that vacations are good for our well-being is one thing; actually taking them seriously is another.

  What if skipping your vacation wasn't just bad for your health, but also bad for your wallet?

  A study involving more than 3,300 men and women found that every additional 10 days of paid vacation was associated with a **29% lower risk of depression among women**. For mothers, the association was even stronger, with a **36% lower risk of depression** for every additional 10 days of paid leave.

  Another well-known long-term study reached a similarly striking conclusion. Men who regularly skipped their annual vacations had a **32% higher risk of dying from a heart attack** compared with those who took vacations regularly.

  Given that, on average, it costs between **€3,000 in Spain** and **€5,000 in Germany** to treat depression (and likely much more in the United States), taking care of yourself and taking regular vacations is an investment in your future mental well-being that could also save you money.

  These numbers are a reminder that a long vacation is more than just a chance to travel or relax. It is an opportunity for your body and mind to recover from work-related stress.

  You don't need to use many vacation days to enjoy longer breaks.   Don't wait until you're exhausted to take a break. Your future self will thank you.
