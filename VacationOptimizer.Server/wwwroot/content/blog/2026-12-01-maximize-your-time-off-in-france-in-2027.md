  France has 11 jours fériés in 2027, and the calendar is unusually generous. With five well-placed days of congés, you can take two proper breaks: nine days in May and four days in November. That is 13 days off for five PTO days — the kind of maths that makes "les ponts" worth planning early.

  There is a catch. France does not generally move a public holiday that falls on a weekend. In 2027, 1 May, 8 May, 15 August and 25 December all land on a Saturday or Sunday, so those dates are simply lost. The wins come from the midweek holidays — Ascension, Whit Monday, Bastille Day, Toussaint and Armistice — where a single bridge day unlocks a much longer break.

  Unlike the UK and the US, France does not roll a weekend holiday to the nearest weekday. When 1 May, 8 May, 15 August or 25 December lands on a weekend, there is no substitute day. Build your plan around the midweek dates instead.

  ## How to turn 5 PTO days into 13 days off in 2027

  Set five days in the [France planner](https://longvacation.eu/app?country=FR&amp;year=2027) and lock these dates:

  - **Monday 3, Tuesday 4, Wednesday 5 and Friday 7 May** — the Thursday 6 May Ascension holiday, the Saturday 1 May and Saturday 8 May holidays, and the weekend do the rest. You are off from Saturday 1 May to Sunday 9 May: **nine consecutive days for four PTO days**.
  - **Friday 12 November** — Armistice Day falls on Thursday 11 November, so one bridge day gives you **four days off (11–14 November)**.

  That is **13 days off for five PTO days**. Whit Monday (17 May) also gives you a free three-day weekend (15–17 May) without spending any leave.

  Want a longer November? Take 2–5 November, 8–10 November and 12 November (eight days) and you are off from Saturday 30 October to Sunday 14 November — **16 consecutive days**. It is the single biggest block in the French 2027 calendar.

  ## Best ponts and long-weekend windows in 2027

  - **May is the main event.** Four days (3–5 and 7 May) turn the two Saturday holidays and Ascension into nine days off (1–9 May). If you can spare nine days, take 3–5, 7 and 10–14 May and connect straight through Whit Monday: **17 days off (1–17 May)**. Request this window first — it is the most competitive leave period of the year.
  - **Armistice Day is the cheapest bridge.** One day on Friday 12 November gives four days off (11–14 November). With eight days you can stretch it to 16 days (30 October–14 November).
  - **Bastille Day is a midweek gift.** The 14 July holiday falls on a Wednesday. Take Thursday 15 and Friday 16 July for five days off (14–18 July), or take Monday 12 and Tuesday 13 July for five days on the other side (10–14 July).
  - **Easter Monday already gives you a free three-day weekend.** Take Tuesday 30 March to Friday 2 April (four days) and the break runs from Saturday 27 March to Sunday 4 April — nine days. In Alsace-Moselle, Good Friday (26 March) is also a public holiday, so the same break can start a day earlier.
  - **Christmas needs five days for nine.** Christmas Day is a Saturday. Take Monday 27 to Friday 31 December and, because New Year's Day 2028 falls on a Saturday, you are off from Saturday 25 December to Sunday 2 January — nine days. Add Friday 24 December for a 10-day reset.
  - **New Year's Day is a Friday.** Take Monday 4 to Friday 8 January (five days) for a 10-day start to the year (1–10 January), or just Monday 4 and Tuesday 5 for a five-day break.

  ## What is a pont — and do you have to work on one?

  A **pont** is the working day between a public holiday and a weekend (or between two public holidays). The French labour code does not give you the day automatically: you either use a day of leave, or your employer grants it through a collective agreement, a company shutdown, or a formal "jour de pont" policy. Some employers make it compulsory to take leave on a pont; others offer it as an extra day. Check your contract or ask HR before you assume a pont is free.

  It also helps to know that not all jours fériés are equal. **1 May** is the only public holiday that is legally non-working and paid for every employee. The other dates depend on your collective agreement, even though most French offices close on all 11 national holidays. If you work in essential services, retail or hospitality, confirm your sector's rules before booking.

  ## Frequently asked questions

  ### Which regions get extra public holidays in France?

  Alsace-Moselle has two extra jours fériés: Good Friday and 26 December (Saint Stephen's Day). In 2027, Good Friday falls on 26 March, so it can extend the Easter weekend; 26 December falls on a Sunday. The planner uses the national French calendar, so add regional dates as custom free days if you work there.

  ### Do I have to work on a pont?

  Not automatically. A pont is a normal working day unless your employer closes the office or grants the day through an agreement. Many French companies let employees take it as leave, and some make it compulsory to do so. Check your contract, collective agreement or HR policy before counting a pont as a free day.

  ### What happens when a public holiday falls on a weekend?

  France does not generally substitute a weekday for a weekend holiday. In 2027, 1 May, 8 May, 15 August and 25 December fall on weekends, so they do not create extra days off. The planner treats them as weekend days; focus on the midweek holidays such as Ascension, Whit Monday, Bastille Day and Armistice Day.

  ### How fast do the May dates fill up?

  May is the busiest leave period in France, especially around Ascension and the Whit Monday weekend. Request the 3–7 May window as early as possible, and check school-holiday dates if you have children. The 11–14 November bridge is easier to secure, but it also fills up once colleagues spot the four-day break.

  ### How many vacation days do French workers get?

  A full-time employee gets five weeks of paid leave per year — 30 working days, or 25 working days in companies that count Monday to Friday — and many companies add RTT days on top. The planner defaults to 25 days, so set your real allowance. With 25 days, the best 2027 plan reaches **51 days off**; 20 days reaches 44, and 30 days reaches 58.
